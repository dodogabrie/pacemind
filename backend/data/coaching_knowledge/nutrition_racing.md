# Race-Day and Training Nutrition

## Key Principles
- Separate daily nutrition thinking from during-exercise fueling — different rules apply
- For runs under 90 minutes: no fueling needed (glycogen stores are sufficient)
- For runs over 90 minutes: must refuel or risk bonking
- Always carry at least 1 gel and 16-20 oz water on trail runs, even short ones (trails are unpredictable)
- Post-exercise nutrition window (first 30 min) is critical — missing it can delay recovery by days
- "Do what works" — eat whatever sustains your energy and feels good in your gut

## Pre-Run Fueling

### Short Runs (<90 min)
- Run on empty stomach — save breakfast for after
- If hungry: eat something small and easy to digest (half banana)
- Coffee or water to wake up and trigger bowel movement
- Glycogen stores are sufficient for 90+ min of moderate running in well-trained athletes
- Less fit runners may need fuel after only 45 minutes

### Long Runs (>90 min)
- Eat breakfast 2-3 hours before: complex carbs + moderate protein
- Example: oatmeal with banana, peanut butter, and honey
- Keep it familiar — nothing new before an important run
- If starting early morning: small easy snack (banana, toast with PB) 30-60 min before
- For afternoon runs: eat lighter-than-usual lunch well in advance

## During-Run Fueling

### Calorie Requirements
- Runners burn approximately 80-100 calories per mile on trails (varies with body weight and terrain)
- Can only absorb 200-300 calories per hour during exercise (stomach can't process more)
- For runs over 90 min: aim for 100-250 calories per hour depending on intensity and duration
- Higher effort = harder to digest solid food → rely more on simple carbs (gels, sports drink)
- Lower effort/conversational pace = can handle solid food and variety

### Fueling Strategy by Distance
**Half-marathon trail race:** 1-3 gels, carried in pockets; may not need to eat at all if well-fueled beforehand
**Trail marathon:** 3-5 gels or equivalent; may benefit from small amounts of solid food at aid stations
**50K:** Similar to marathon; 75% of estimated finish time in long training run with practiced fueling
**50+ miles:** Must eat substantial food — trail mix, PB&J, wraps, quesadillas, soup; train your gut for this
**100 miles:** Variety is key — alternate sweet and salty, simple and complex; expect cravings to guide you

### What to Eat During Ultras
**Competitive/high effort (heart rate too high for conversation):**
- Primarily quick-burning, easily digestible carbs: gels, sports drinks, Coke
- Example: Magdalena Boulet won Western States 100 on GU Roctane drink only

**Conversational pace (majority of ultrarunners):**
- Mix of fuel sources, especially past lunchtime when real food cravings hit
- Good options: trail mix, half PB&J sandwich, turkey/avocado wrap, energy bars, quesadillas, soup, noodles
- Nibble slowly over miles rather than eating large amounts at once
- Some thrive on junk food (Clare Gallagher won Leadville 100 on cake frosting and candy)
- Some thrive on healthy plant-based fare

## Post-Run Recovery Nutrition
- **Timing:** Consume 100-200 calories within 30 minutes post-exercise for runs >1 hour
- **Ratio:** 3:1 or 4:1 carbohydrates to protein
- **Why critical:** Muscles can rapidly replenish glycogen stores during this window; missing it delays recovery by days
- **Examples:**
  - Recovery drink mix with carbs + protein + electrolytes
  - Hardboiled egg + fruit + vanilla milk
  - Small smoothie with protein powder
  - Huevos rancheros (eggs, beans, avocado, salsa) — 50g carbs, 32g protein, 19g fat, ~475 cal
- **Higher intensity workout = more post-run hunger** — body continues burning calories
- **Lower intensity = less post-run hunger** — don't gorge thinking you "earned" it
- **Common mistake:** Socializing at finish line and forgetting to eat → feeling thrashed for days

## Hydration and Electrolytes

### General Guidelines
- Carry at least 16-20 oz water for any trail run
- Drink according to thirst — don't force excessive fluid intake
- In most cases: carry plain water + separate source of calories with electrolytes
- Sports drinks can backfire: may drink too much for calories, get queasy, then stop drinking → dehydration

### Two Serious Hydration Mistakes
**1. Dehydration + low electrolytes:**
- Signs: dark urine, long time without peeing, losing ability to sweat, dizziness, >2% weight loss
- Action: consume sports drink (salts + sugars + fluid); cool off
- Risk: heat exhaustion → potentially fatal heatstroke

**2. Overhydration (hyponatremia):**
- Signs: weight gain, puffiness during run, nausea, "sloshy stomach," irritability, confusion, clear urine
- Cause: too much plain water without electrolytes
- Action: STOP drinking, stop running, rest, eat salty snack (banana dipped in table salt)
- Risk: potentially fatal — can cause seizures and neurological problems
- If mental impairment + swollen neck/cheeks → get medical attention immediately

## Daily Nutrition for Trail Runners

### 8 Principles for Healthy Balanced Eating
1. Make protein a focus of every meal (meat, plant, and dairy sources)
2. Eat moderate high-quality unrefined carbs (colorful veggies, whole grains, fruit)
3. Eat moderate healthy fats (avocado, olives, nuts — fats liquid at room temp)
4. Eat minimally processed foods
5. Make lunch your heartiest meal; moderate alcohol, avoid overeating at dinner
6. Get enough iron from real food (not supplements)
7. Moderate sodium intake
8. Eat mindfully — slowly, with minimal distractions, honoring hunger/fullness cues (4-6 on scale of 1-10)

### Iron for Trail Runners
- Iron-deficiency anemia causes unusual slowness, sluggishness, energy drain during runs
- Ferritin/iron plays role in hemoglobin (O2 transport) and metabolism
- Heme iron (from meat) is much easier to absorb than plant-based iron
- Enhance absorption: consume vitamin C with iron-rich foods; cook in cast-iron pans
- Get blood checked when healthy to establish baseline, not just when sick
- Avoid iron supplements unless medically necessary (risk of hemochromatosis in some people)

## Decision Rules for the Agent
- IF run <90 min THEN no fueling needed; run on empty stomach with water
- IF run 90 min - 3 hours THEN take 2-4 gels and water; eat 100-200 cal/hour
- IF run >3 hours THEN practice with solid food; 200-300 cal/hour; alternate sweet/salty
- IF athlete reports bonking in training THEN check pre-run nutrition and glycogen replenishment from prior day
- IF athlete reports GI issues during runs THEN simplify to gels + water; avoid eating <1 hour before running; test different products
- IF athlete reports post-race exhaustion lasting days THEN check post-run nutrition timing — likely missing the 30-min recovery window
- IF athlete has clear urine + nausea + puffiness during run THEN WARNING: possible hyponatremia — stop drinking, eat salty food, seek medical help
- IF athlete reports chronic fatigue + sluggishness THEN recommend blood panel including ferritin
