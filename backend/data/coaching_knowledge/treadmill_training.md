# Treadmill Training for Trail Races

For athletes who can run outdoors only once or twice a week. A treadmill
trains the uphill well and the downhill not at all: plan around that gap.

## What transfers and what does not
- **Aerobic work transfers.** Easy runs, threshold and intervals on the treadmill
  build the same engine as outdoors.
- **Climbing transfers.** Incline loads the same muscles as an outdoor climb, at a
  pace and grade you control exactly (useful for keeping HR in zone).
- **Descent does not transfer.** Most home treadmills have no decline. Downhill
  running is eccentric work for the quadriceps: it causes the muscle damage and
  soreness that slow runners in the second half of a hilly race, and only prior
  downhill / eccentric exposure protects against it (the repeated-bout effect).
  A treadmill-heavy plan without outdoor descents arrives at race day with
  unprepared quads.

## Setting the treadmill
- **Easy and steady runs at 1 % incline.** A 1 % grade matches the energy cost of
  running outdoors, mainly at faster paces (above ~15 km/h); at easy paces 0-1 %
  makes no measurable difference (Jones & Doust 1996).
- **Climbs at the machine's max incline (e.g. 10 %).** At 10 % every km gives
  100 m of D+. Above ~12-15 % most runners switch to power hiking; with a 10 % cap,
  practise hiking with hands on thighs at the top speed you would use on the race's
  steepest sections.
- **Long climbs, not only short reps.** Race climbs last 20-40 min: alternate short
  hill repeats with sustained 15-30 min blocks at max incline in Z2-Z3.
- **Lower the incline to recover between reps** instead of stepping off.

## Covering the descent without a decline
1. **Put the descent on the outdoor days.** The weekday outdoor run and the
   weekend long run must include real downhill, progressively steeper and longer.
   The plan engine places descent_session only on non-treadmill days.
2. **Repeat the stimulus regularly.** A first bout of downhill running protects
   against damage from the next ones; the effect is partial and grows with repeated
   sessions. Ten eccentric-biased sessions reduced quadriceps soreness after a
   standard downhill run, but did not change the neuromuscular fatigue it causes
   (Bontemps et al. 2024): the descents in training reduce soreness, they do not
   make the race descent free.
3. **Eccentric strength 1-2x/week.** Slow step-downs from a box, split squats and
   squats with a 3-4 s lowering phase, Nordic-style reverse lunges. They complement
   but do not replace running downhill: none of them reproduces the impact and
   speed of a technical descent.
4. **Do not stack it before the race.** Muscle damage from a big descent takes days
   to clear: no hard downhill in the last 7-10 days before the race.

## Caveats
- Evidence on how much eccentric gym work protects downhill running is limited and
  mixed: compression garments, special shoes and stride changes have no proven
  protective effect (Bontemps et al. 2020).
- The 1 % rule comes from 9 trained men over 6-min bouts; treat it as a guide.
