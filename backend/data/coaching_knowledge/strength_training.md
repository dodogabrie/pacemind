# Strength Training for Mountain Runners

## Key Principles
- Athletes strength train to improve event performance and prevent injury — NOT to get stronger in the gym
- Endurance sports use only ~25% of max single-rep strength, but for thousands of repetitions
- Gaining strength WITHOUT adding muscle mass is critical — you carry that muscle up mountains
- Muscular endurance is more important than pure strength for all events covered
- Specific strength correlates with performance better than general strength

## General vs Specific Strength

### General Strength
**What:** Non-sport-specific exercises (squats, push-ups, pull-ups) that build a base for specific work
**When:** Transition Period and early Base Period
**How much is enough:** No definitive answer, but:
- If you can't half-squat your body weight: you'd benefit from more general strength
- If you can squat 2x body weight: likely excessive — spend time on specific strength instead
- If you can't do a single pull-up (skimo): gains available from improving this

### Specific Strength
**What:** Exercises that mimic sport movement patterns, speeds, and forces
**Primary exercises:** Hill Sprints and Hill Bounding
**Why specific > general:** The brain's muscle-recruitment patterns are learned skills — must train sport-specific contraction speeds and patterns, not just slow heavy lifting

## Strength Assessment Protocol

### Leg/Hip Assessment (3 tests in order of difficulty)
1. **Air Squat Test:** Watch if knees collapse inward → if yes, start Stage 1
2. **Box Step Test:** Step onto knee-height box watching knee tracking → if >1 inch inward collapse, Stage 2
3. **Sit-to-Stand Test:** Rise from seated position on one leg → if >1 inch knee collapse, Stage 3; if passed, go to specific strength

### Core Assessment
- **Plank:** <30 sec = Stage 1; 30-60 sec = Stage 2; >60 sec = Stage 3
- **Windshield Wiper:** 0 reps straight legs = Stage 1; <5 = Stage 2; >5 = Stage 3

### Upper Body Assessment
- **Push-up:** Can't hold plank 5 sec = Stage 1; <5 push-ups = Stage 2; >5 push-ups = Stage 3

## General Strength Programs

### Stage 1 (Remedial)
- Squats with knee band: 4x20 reps
- Single-leg lateral band: 4x20 reps
- Intersperse upper-body sets between leg sets
- 1 min rest between sets
- Retest knee alignment after 2 weeks

### Stage 2 (Building)
- Box step-ups: 3x15 per leg (body weight)
- Box step-downs: 3x15 per leg
- Front lunges: 3x15
- Progress to 3x30 with perfect form before moving to Stage 3
- 1-2 min rest between sets

### Stage 3 (Loading)
- Same exercises as Stage 2 but with added weight (vest, pack, barbell)
- Select weight allowing exactly 8 reps with perfect form
- 4x6 reps with weight
- 2 min rest between sets

### Core Exercises
- **Plank:** Progress from knees to toes to weighted
- **Windshield Wiper:** Progress from bent knees to straight legs to ankle weights
- **Kayaker:** Seated rotation with heavy dumbbell
- **Standing Trunk Rotation:** Fast, aggressive rotation — the stopping/starting motion provides the effect
- **Marching Plank:** Lift each limb in succession, hold until form breaks

## Hill Sprints / Hill Bounding (Specific Strength)

### Protocol
**Gradient:** 30-50% grade (17-27 degrees); steeper is better for strength
**Duration per rep:** 8-10 seconds MAX (do NOT extend)
**Rest between reps:** 2 minutes walk down (minimum)
**Rest between sets:** 3 minutes
**Starting volume:** 6-8 reps
**Progression:** Add no more than 2 reps per week

### Warm-Up (Critical!)
1. 20 min running warm-up ending with 2-3 min Z3 (must break a sweat)
2. 10 min dynamic stretching: toe bouncing, lunges, leg swings, ballistic toe touches
3. 2x20-30 sec medium-to-hard uphill runs (1-2 min walk recovery)
4. 2x20 sec skipping uphill (1-2 min walk recovery)

### Key Points
- Hill Sprints = running technique uphill at maximum speed
- Hill Bounding = exaggerated stride length, more time airborne, lower tempo
- First several workouts may not feel tiring (using ST fibers) — this means you need it MORE
- Endurance athletes hate the long rests — the extended rests ARE the training
- Start at 80-85% effort for first 2-3 workouts to avoid injury
- Include in Base Period 1x/week if general strength is adequate

## Muscular Endurance (ME)

### What It Is
**Definition:** Ability to maintain relatively high force output over many repetitions — the boundary between strength and endurance
**Why it matters:** For ultrarunners, decrease in stride length during races is one of the most powerful predictors of results
**The Frontier Fiber Model:** At any speed, the "frontier fibers" (highest-power fibers recruited for that speed) determine endurance at that speed. ME training improves their endurance.

### Do You Need More ME?
**Signs you're limited by muscular endurance:**
- Legs give out before breathing during workouts
- Slowing in workouts under 2 hours (not glycogen depletion)
- Heavy, dead legs that lose their bounce
- Low-level burning and acute fatigue in legs during high intensity
- Downhills beat up your legs for days
- HR drops despite increased effort (frontier fibers have dropped out)

### Gym-Based ME Workout
**Exercises (in order):**
1. Box step-ups: 6x10 per leg
2. Front lunges: 6x10 per leg
3. Split jump squats: 6x10 per leg
4. Squat jumps: 6x10
- 1 min rest between sets, 2 min between exercises
- Tempo: ~1 rep/second

**Progression:**
- Workouts 1-2: Body weight only
- Workouts 3-4: Add 10% body weight
- Workout 5: 8 sets, 10% body weight
- Workouts 6-7: 6 sets, 10% BW, 30 sec rest
- Workout 8: 8 sets, 10% BW, 30 sec rest
- Continue: increase weight to 15%, then 20% with adjusted rest
- Space 7-12 days apart; if >12 days gap, repeat previous workout

### Outdoor ME Workout
**Method 1 — Steep hill Z3 grinding:**
- Find 20-50% gradient hill
- Start: 2x 8-10 min uphill with low-level leg burn, 5 min moving recovery
- Build to 40 min of steady uphill grinding

**Method 2 — Bounding variation:**
- Start: 10x10 sec bounding up steep trail, walk down recovery
- Progress to 15x15 sec bounding with 1 min recovery
- Then 20x20 sec with 2 min recovery

**Method 3 — Weighted pack:**
- Add 10-15% body weight in a pack
- Use either Z3 grinding or bounding on less steep terrain
- Fire stairs in tall buildings are excellent for this

**Frequency:** 1x per week to 10 days during Base Period
**Key rule:** Maintain high Z1-Z2 volume during ME phase
**Recovery:** Expect sore legs for 2-3 days; separate from Z3/Z4 by 72 hours

## Strength in the Training Plan
- **Transition Period:** General strength 2x/week, Core 2x/week
- **Early Base Period:** General or Max strength 2x/week, Core 2x/week, introduce Hill Sprints 1x/week
- **Mid-Late Base Period:** Shift to ME workouts 1x/week; maintain Hill Sprints 1x/week; Core 2x/week
- **Precompetition:** Reduce ME as Z3/Z4 increases; maintain Core 1-2x/week; maintain Hill Sprints
- **Competition:** 1 Core/week; cut Hill Sprints 30%/week during taper; drop ME
- **Goal Week:** No strength training

## Common Mistakes
- Spending too much time on general strength at the expense of specific training
- Getting "gym strong" with bulky muscles that add mass without proportional performance benefit
- Skipping the long rests during Hill Sprints (endurance athletes' #1 complaint)
- Overusing ME training — if no improvement week-to-week, doing too much, not too little
- Starting ME too aggressively — begin with no added weight, progress very gradually

## Decision Rules for the Agent
- IF athlete fails Air Squat knee tracking THEN prescribe Stage 1 general strength (remedial)
- IF athlete reports knee pain or poor knee tracking THEN prioritize hip stabilization exercises before anything else
- IF athlete reports legs failing before breathing THEN prescribe ME work (gym-based or outdoor)
- IF athlete's event is <2 hours THEN muscular strength plays larger role; if >6 hours, ME dominates
- IF athlete is new to ME training THEN start with body weight only, 1x/week, expect soreness
- IF athlete sees no ME improvement week-over-week THEN reduce frequency (doing too much)
- IF time is limited THEN prioritize ME over general strength; Hill Sprints over gym work
