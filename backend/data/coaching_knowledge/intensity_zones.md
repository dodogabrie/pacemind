# Training Intensity Zones

## Key Principles
- The zone system is anchored by two metabolic markers: Aerobic Threshold (AeT) and Lactate Threshold (LT)
- Zones are personalized — percentage-of-max-HR formulas are less accurate than metabolic-marker-based zones
- Metabolic response varies day to day (recovery state is the biggest factor) — zones are guidelines, not rigid boundaries
- Heart rate is the only practical intensity measure for uphill athletes (terrain variability makes pace unreliable)
- The 5-zone system is the most time-tested and practically useful model

## The Five Training Zones

### Recovery Zone (below Zone 1)
**What:** Very light exercise for speeding recovery
**HR:** At least 20% below upper Zone 1
**Duration:** 15 min to 1 hour, highly individual
**Purpose:** Speeds recovery from demanding sessions; should feel better afterward
**Lactate:** <1 mMol/L
**Modes:** Walking, easy jog, swimming, cycling — cross-training especially beneficial

### Zone 1: Aerobic Conditioning
**What:** Foundation of aerobic development
**HR:** Up to ~10% below AeT
**Lactate:** <1 to ~1.5 mMol/L
**Duration:** Minimum 30 min continuous, up to several hours
**Annual volume share:** 50-70% of all aerobic training
**Training effects:** Increased blood volume, mitochondrial mass, capillary density, aerobic enzymes, fat metabolism
**Muscle fibers:** Predominantly slow-twitch (ST)

### Zone 2: Aerobic Capacity
**What:** Maximum aerobic system output — the key to endurance success
**HR:** Up to AeT (where blood lactate reaches ~2 mMol/L)
**Duration:** 30-120 minutes continuous
**Training effects:** Greatest effect on raising AeT; same as Z1 but accrues to larger pool of muscle fibers
**Important caveat:** Athletes with high AeT (within 10% of LT) should limit Z2 to 1-2 days/week and <10-15% of weekly volume — pace is fast enough to be taxing
**For ADS athletes:** Can do almost all training in Z2 initially because pace is slow

### Zone 3: Endurance
**What:** Hard aerobic training between AeT and LT
**HR:** Between AeT and LT
**Lactate:** Elevated but sustainable for up to 1 hour
**Annual volume share:** No more than 10% of total annual volume
**Training effects:** Powerful stimulus to aerobic and glycolytic systems; strong muscular endurance effect
**Warning:** Most commonly overused intensity — seductive because gains come fast, but overuse leads to performance decline
**When to add:** Only after AeT is within 10% of LT (Ten Percent Test)

### Zone 4: Aerobic Power (VO2max)
**What:** Above-LT intensity that maximally utilizes aerobic and glycolytic systems
**HR:** ~90-95% of maximum heart rate
**Duration:** Work periods 30 sec to 8 min; total work volume 15-30 min
**Format:** Interval style with 1:1 work-to-rest ratio; active recovery between reps
**Training effects:** Rapid fitness gains; improves lactate shuttle mechanism; strong muscular endurance effect
**Recovery needed:** 72 hours between Z4 sessions; separate from Z3 sessions
**Prerequisite:** Sufficient muscular endurance — if legs give out before breathing, Z4 is not effective

### Zone 5: Maximum Anaerobic Effort
**What:** Maximum intensity sustainable for <30-45 seconds
**HR:** Not a good measure due to cardiac lag
**Duration:** 8-15 seconds per rep with very long rest (3+ min)
**Rest ratio:** 4:1 to 10:1 rest-to-work
**Training effects:** Improves sport-specific strength, technique, anaerobic power, economy
**When to include:** Can be used in most training periods; early in cycle as 10-sec sprints, later extended for endurance effect
**Injury risk:** High — requires good strength base

## The Ten Percent Test
**What:** Determines if aerobic base is sufficient to add Z3+ training
**How:**
1. Determine AeT HR (via lab test, nose breathing test, or MAF)
2. Do LT test (30-60 min sustained max uphill effort; average HR = LT)
3. Calculate: LT HR / AeT HR
**Interpretation:**
- Ratio > 1.10 (>10% spread): Still have ADS — focus on Z1-2 base building, limit Z3
- Ratio 1.06-1.10 (6-10% spread): Can add Z3 workouts
- Ratio < 1.06 (elite-level): Replace Z2 with Z3, can use more Z4
**Example:** AeT HR 128, LT HR 150 → 150/128 = 1.17 → 17% spread → significant ADS, needs base work

## Polarized Training Distribution
**What:** How world-class endurance athletes actually train (Seiler's research)
**Distribution across sports (XC skiing, running, cycling, rowing):**
- 80% below AeT (Zones 1-2)
- 6-8% between AeT and LT (Zone 3)
- 12-14% above LT (Zones 4-5)
**Key insight:** This is the opposite of what popular fitness media advises — elites do very little "moderate" intensity

## AeT Testing Methods (in order of ease)

### 1. MAF Method (simplest)
**Formula:** 180 - age (with modifiers)
- Recovering from injury/long layoff or sick >2-3x/year: subtract 5
- Training >2 years with regular progress: add 5
**Limitation:** Cannot track AeT increases over time (fixed formula)

### 2. Ventilatory Marker (field test)
**How:** HR at which you can just barely maintain nose-only breathing or conversation
**Accuracy:** Good correlation with 2 mMol/L blood lactate in moderate-to-well-trained athletes
**Limitation:** Does not work for untrained or HIIT-dominated backgrounds

### 3. Lactate Step Test (DIY lab test)
**How:** 15 min warmup → series of 3-min stages at increasing speed (10 bpm per stage) → blood lactate sample at end of each stage
**AeT reached when:** Lactate reaches or exceeds 2 mMol/L, or first stage where lactate increases by 1 mMol/L
**Equipment:** Portable lactate analyzer (~$200)

### 4. Gas Exchange Test (gold standard)
**How:** Lab test on treadmill with metabolic cart; determines AeT, LT, VO2max, fat/carb ratio at all intensities
**Cost:** $150-350
**Caveat:** Many labs only test for VO2max and skip AeT — insist on at least 3-min stages to properly determine AeT

## LT Testing
**How:** Maximum sustained uphill effort for 30-60 min (30 min for less trained, 60 for well-trained)
**Prep:** Good warmup (15-20 min), fresh legs (reduce training load for a few days)
**Execution:** Settle into max sustainable pace; HR will stabilize in a narrow range; average HR = LT

## Common Mistakes
- Using percentage-of-max-HR zones instead of metabolically-based zones
- Overusing Zone 3 because it feels productive ("one-stop shopping" mentality)
- Ignoring the Ten Percent Test and adding intensity too early
- Expecting false precision from zones — a 7-zone system is not more accurate than a 5-zone system
- Using short-stage VO2max tests to determine AeT (stages too short for aerobic system to stabilize)

## Decision Rules for the Agent
- IF athlete has no lab data THEN use ventilatory test (nose breathing) for AeT and 30-min hill test for LT
- IF AeT/LT spread >10% THEN limit Z3 to 0, prescribe Z1-Z2 base building
- IF AeT/LT spread 6-10% THEN allow 1 Z3 session/week (5% of weekly volume initially)
- IF AeT/LT spread <6% THEN drop Z2, replace with Z3; can add Z4 when Z3 reaches 10% of volume
- IF athlete reports legs failing before breathing in Z4 THEN prescribe muscular endurance work before more Z4
- ALWAYS separate Z3 and Z4 sessions by at least 72 hours
