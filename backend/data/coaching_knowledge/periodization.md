# Periodization and Training Planning

## Key Principles
- Periodization organizes training into blocks with distinct aims — focusing on few qualities at a time yields better results
- Capacity training (building potential) must precede Utilization training (exploiting potential)
- The three pillars of successful training: Continuity, Gradualness, Modulation
- A well-coached but less-than-ideal plan gives better results than the best plan poorly administered
- The plan is a suggestion, not a blueprint — blind adherence leads to inferior results

## Capacity vs Utilization Training

### Capacity Training (Bob Bowman model)
**What:** Training that improves long-term performance potential
**When:** Prioritized during Base Period
**Characteristics:**
- Gains take a long time to accrue but are long-lasting
- Trains qualities in isolation (appears less sport-specific)
- May even reduce near-term performance
- Like building an Interstate highway — slow construction but allows much more traffic later
**Types:**
- Aerobic Capacity: Z1-Z2 training (top priority — can never have too much)
- Anaerobic Capacity: Glycolytic metabolism (can have too much — event dependent)
- Strength Capacity: Max force and local muscular endurance
- Technical Capacity: Economy of locomotion, balance, movement patterning

### Utilization Training
**What:** Training that improves near-term performance results
**When:** Prioritized before competition
**Characteristics:**
- Quick-acting but effects are volatile and short-lived
- Must be sport/event specific for maximum effect
- Reduces capacity — must be offset with capacity maintenance
- Like dumping traffic on the highway — works only if highway is built
**Types:**
- Aerobic Utilization: Z3-Z5 training (LT, VO2max work)
- Anaerobic Utilization: Training at 5-10% faster than race pace
**Warning:** Back-to-back awesome and horrible races = too much utilization, not enough capacity

## Training Periods

### Transition Period (2-8 weeks)
**Purpose:** Prepare body for Base Period training
**Who needs it:**
- New to structured training: 8 weeks
- Coming off competition season: 2-3 weeks
- Returning after significant break: 4-6 weeks
**Starting volume:**
- After 4+ week break: 50% of previous cycle's average weekly hours
- After 2-week sport transition: 80% of recent volume
**Guidelines:**
- Should NOT feel challenging in early weeks
- 1 long aerobic workout/week (25% of weekly volume) at Z1 or Z2 (depending on ADS status)
- 1-2 general strength sessions/week
- Fill remaining volume with easy aerobic exercise
- All work should be foot-borne
- Don't accelerate load >7-10%/week

### Base Period (months)
**Purpose:** Increase work capacity and fatigue resistance — the most crucial work
**Focus:** Aerobic base building, strength development, muscular endurance
**Key points:**
- >80% of time in Z1-Z2
- Performance will be masked by low-level fatigue (expected)
- Don't expect best results during this period
- C-level races only (minimal disruption to training progression)
- Cannot effectively build capacity AND utilize it simultaneously
**Weekly structure:**
- 1 long aerobic workout (30-40% of weekly volume)
- 2 strength sessions (general or specific)
- 2 core strength workouts
- Fill remaining with Z1-Z2 aerobic work
- Active recovery work at least 3x/week

### Precompetition Period (weeks)
**Purpose:** Add event-specific training on top of base
**What changes:**
- Add Z3 workouts (start with 5% of weekly volume, build to 10%)
- After Z3 is comfortable, replace some with Z4
- For ultras: back-to-back long runs
- For short races (VK): double Z4 sessions
- Reduce ME training as Z3/Z4 increases
**Duration:** Measured in weeks, not months

### Competition Period
**Purpose:** Utilize capacity built during Base + Precomp
**Guidelines:**
- Racing maximally utilizes all capacity — recovery needed between events
- 2-3 weeks between shorter races, 4-6 weeks between ultras
- If possible, insert training blocks of 2-3 weeks between key races
- Maintain aerobic base even during race season (hardest challenge)
- A races: full taper; B races: slight load reduction; C races: train through

## The Three Training Principles

### Continuity
- Maintain regular training schedule with minimal interruption
- Occasional 1-2 day breaks are fine if <5% of monthly volume
- Breaks >5% of monthly volume require stepping back in the plan
- Cannot pretend missed training happened — body is not prepared for next step

### Gradualness
- Training loads must gradually increase to have beneficial effect
- Beginners (<350-400 hrs/year): can increase up to 25%/year
- Advanced (>500 hrs/year): max 10% increase/year
- Elite: often no change in annual volume — gains from juggling intensity/specificity
- Infrequent bludgeoning is never as effective as frequent gentle nudges

### Modulation
- Fluctuating training load allowing recovery between build-up periods
- Typical pattern: 3 weeks build : 1 week recovery (3:1)
- Some athletes do better with 2:1 — experiment to find what works
- Recovery weeks: drop load 40-60% of last big week
- Overreaching (advanced): planned short period of very high load followed by substantial recovery

## Load Progression Guidelines
- Max 10% increase/week in distance, vertical, or time
- Don't increase distance AND vertical AND intensity in same week — max 2, keep <15% combined
- Don't increase >15% for two weeks in a row
- After near-15% jump, follow with recovery week
- Don't increase for more than 3 weeks in a row
- One long workout/week = 30-40% of weekly distance/vertical

## Intensity Introduction Guidelines
- Start with 1 Z3 workout/week when handling Z1-Z2 volume well
- Begin Z3 with 20-30 min total (e.g., 3x7-8 min, 3:1 work:rest)
- Build Z3 gradually — max 10%/week increase in Z3 volume
- When Z3 reaches 10% of weekly volume and feels comfortable, begin adding Z4
- Add Z4 by replacing Z3 volume: start with 5% of weekly volume in Z4
- Drop Z2 when adding Z3/Z4 — shift base work to Z1
- If adding intensity forces >5% drop in Z1-Z2 volume, you're adding too much
- New to Z4: start with 30/30s before progressing to longer intervals
- Separate Z3 and Z4 sessions by at least 72 hours

## Race Planning
### Priority System
- **A priority:** Main focus — significant taper, full utilization, significant recovery after
- **B priority:** Tune-ups/tests — slight load reduction before/after, test fitness/strategy/equipment
- **C priority:** Train through — just another workout, no plan adjustment needed

### Taper Guidelines
- A races: more dramatic taper; B races: lighter taper; C races: no taper
- Reduce volume gradually, maintain some intensity for sharpening
- Cut overall intensity volume ~30%/week during taper
- Nothing new during taper week — nothing will make you faster, many things will make you slower
- Stay active daily during taper to avoid sluggishness
- Goal week: Recovery Zone only, max 4 days, with 3-4 x 30-sec accelerations in 2 workouts

## Athlete Categories

### Category 1 (Building a base)
**Criteria (any of):**
- Less than 400 hours/year of focused training in past 2 years
- Under age 18
- Not successfully completed target-distance events in past 12 months
**Priority:** Increase training volume; expect multiple cycles in base-building phase

### Category 2 (Maintaining + supplementing base)
**Criteria (all of):**
- Over 400 hours/year consistently for 2+ years
- Over age 18
- Successfully completed target-distance events in past 12 months
- Races multiple times per year
**Priority:** Increase specificity of training; more high-intensity supplementing large base

## Recommended Peak Weekly Distances for Mountain Races
- **50K:** Peak week 80-88 km (50-55 mi); avg biggest 12 weeks = 50 km (36 mi); should be comfortable at 40 km/week before starting plan
- **100K:** Peak week 120-130 km (74-80 mi); avg biggest 16 weeks = 75 km (48 mi); should be comfortable with 50K loads first
- **100 mile:** Peak week 145-160 km (90-100 mi); avg biggest 18 weeks = 100 km (75 mi); should be comfortable with 100K loads first

## Decision Rules for the Agent
- IF athlete has <400 hrs/year training history THEN Category 1 — prioritize volume, limit intensity
- IF athlete has >400 hrs/year for 2+ years THEN Category 2 — can add more specificity and intensity
- IF targeting first race at a new distance THEN add at least one full training cycle of base building at previous distance first
- IF recovery weeks don't feel refreshing by Sunday THEN athlete needs longer recovery or lighter recovery weeks
- IF athlete reports consistent C/D grades on workouts THEN reduce training load, assess recovery
- IF multiple A races in season THEN need base-building blocks (2-3 weeks) between them
- IF race is >4 weeks away THEN still in base/build phase — don't add race-specific work yet
