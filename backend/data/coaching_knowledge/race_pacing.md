# Race Pacing and Execution

## Key Principles
- Pace by feel, not by numbers — trail terrain variability makes consistent pace impossible
- Break the race into thirds: conservative start, steady middle, push finish
- "Nothing new on race day" — test all gear, nutrition, and strategies in training first
- Set three-tiered goals: base (finish/don't blow up), challenging (specific target), stretch (if everything clicks)
- Study the course thoroughly before race day

## The Three-Thirds Pacing Strategy
### First Third: Conservative Start
- Run at a steady, focused, efficient, manageable effort
- Establish rhythm; calm yourself if over-amped
- Don't exceed "comfortably hard" tempo feeling
- The longer the distance, the more conservative the start
- For ultras: should feel almost easy and mellow
- Run YOUR race — don't chase others going out too fast

### Middle Third: Take What the Trail Gives
- Do the best running/hiking the trail presents
- Aim for a feeling of flow; don't push past sustainability
- Maintain focus and efficiency from the start
- Don't succumb to negativity and doubt (common in the middle)
- Expect the unexpected; troubleshoot as needed
- Stay positive, strong, steady

### Final Third: Dig Deep
- Progressively push harder approaching the finish
- Strive to pass others; sustain maximum effort
- Calibrate to highest effort without crossing to unsustainable sprint
- The closer to the finish, the closer to "redlining"
- Use positive self-talk and mantras

## Trail-Specific Pacing Considerations
- Trail pace varies enormously — 7-min/mi on flats to 17-min/mi on steep climbs at the same effort
- Focus on TIME rather than precise mileage for training ("I'll be out for 3 hours")
- GPS watch distance/pace readings are less accurate on hilly routes with switchbacks
- Trail races generally take significantly longer than road races (a 1:30 road half = 2:00+ trail half)

### Estimating Trail Race Finish Time
- Look at prior years' results for similar-level runners
- Run half the race distance on similar terrain, double the time, add 10%
- Be conservative with estimates — trails are slower than you expect

### Going for a Time Goal
- Calculate average pace from goal finish time
- Adjust pace mile-by-mile based on elevation profile
- Example: 4:30 marathon = 10:17/mi average → 10-10:30 on flats, 12:30 on climbs, 8-8:30 on downhills
- Don't pressure yourself to hit specific splits — use ranges, not exact times

## The Hat Brim Trick (Run vs Hike Decision)
**What:** When unsure whether to run or hike an uphill, look at the brim of your hat
- If you can see the trail ahead past the brim: the grade is runnable
- If you can only see the ground at your feet: it's steep enough to hike
**Why:** Provides a quick, intuitive grade assessment without overthinking

## Pace Band for Ultras
**What:** Laminated wristband listing aid stations with fast/slow pace estimates
**How to make:**
1. List all aid stations with mileage
2. Calculate pace to each station for aspirational goal time and base goal time
3. Print as a strip, laminate with packing tape, tape around wrist
4. For 100K/100mi: make two bands (first half + second half)
**Use:** Gauge whether you're on pace; motivate to catch up or confirm you can afford to slow down
**Warning:** Don't let it frustrate you — course conditions may differ from estimates

## Aid Station Strategy
### Guidelines for All Races
1. Make mental checklist BEFORE entering: what do you need? (refill bottle, grab gel, empty trash, apply sunscreen)
2. Be gracious — thank volunteers, don't yell or complain
3. Before exiting: verify you have everything, know which way to go on the trail
4. Don't dawdle or overeat (especially in sub-ultra distances)

### For Sub-Ultra Races
- Likely need only simple carbs (gels, sports drink, soda)
- If grabbing solid food, nibble slowly over next few miles rather than eating it all at once

### For Ultras
- Aid stations become more elaborate (quesadillas, soup, pancakes)
- Walk through while eating to maintain forward movement
- Know what products the race provides; test them in training
- Consider carrying everything and only using stations for water

## Three-Tiered Goal Setting
**How to set goals for a B-level race (example):**
1. **Base goal:** Start conservatively, enjoy the route, avoid blowing up, finish
2. **Challenging but achievable:** Be agile and gutsy on technical terrain, hit specific halfway time, push in second half
3. **Stretch goal:** If goals 1-2 met in first half and conditions are favorable, push for specific finish time

## Race Week Checklist

### Day Before
- Lay out all clothes head to toe
- Pin race bib on shirt (numbers on front)
- Lay out gear, calories, comfort items
- Pack headlamp with fresh batteries if needed
- Prepare drop bag layers
- Charge watch and phone (phone in plastic baggie)
- Check race-day instructions and transportation
- Print course map to carry in baggie
- Pack finish-line bag (towel, change of clothes, recovery snack)
- Trim toenails (ideally a few days before)
- Get breakfast ready; set alarm
- Relax and go to bed confident

### Morning Of
- Wake early enough to eat and digest
- Apply anti-chafe lube; men cover nipples
- Prep feet with antiblister lube
- Fill hydration
- Get dressed, gather everything
- Arrive ~45 min early
- Check in, set positive tone

## Going Off Course
- Extremely common — almost a rite of passage
- Don't blindly follow the runner in front of you
- If off-course: retrace steps to where you went astray (don't shortcut)
- Check course map; wait for other runners if unsure at intersection
- Maintain positive outlook — you still have your base goal

## Decision Rules for the Agent
- IF athlete asks about race pacing THEN recommend thirds strategy: conservative start, steady middle, push finish
- IF athlete has a time goal THEN calculate average pace and show how to adjust per elevation profile
- IF race has significant vert THEN emphasize pacing by feel/effort, not by pace numbers
- IF first trail race THEN emphasize that trail times are 20-40% slower than equivalent road times
- IF ultra distance THEN recommend pace band and three-tiered goal setting
- IF athlete tends to go out too fast THEN strongly emphasize conservative first-third strategy
